Privacy Policy

Last Updated: October 3, 2026

What stays on your Mac

Quill stores the following data locally in ~/Library/Application Support/Quill/:

  • credentials.json — your WordPress site URL, username, and Application Password. Stored as a plain JSON file with restricted permissions (chmod 600). Quill deliberately does not use the macOS system keychain to avoid password prompts when the app relaunches.
  • ai_settings.json — your Anthropic API key, the posts you chose as style samples, the style guide generated from them, and your AI preferences (if you use the AI writing features), also chmod 600.
  • drafts.db — a SQLite database of drafts you've started but not yet saved to WordPress, and autosaved versions of posts and pages you're editing. It is not synced anywhere.

macOS also keeps a few files for Quill in your Library folder: a preferences file with window sizes, your site's address, and any update you chose to skip; and caches of images and web content the editor has loaded. They are listed under Deleting your data.

What Quill sends over the network

Quill connects to three places:

1. Your WordPress site
Every read and write operation (loading posts, saving drafts, publishing, uploading media) goes directly to your WordPress site's REST API using the credentials you provide. Quill is the client; your site is the server. No third party is involved in this traffic. Images in a post load from wherever they are hosted, which is usually your own site.

When you connect, Quill reads your site's public REST API index and loads its site icon, which may be hosted elsewhere, such as on a CDN. It then opens your browser to your site's approval page. WordPress names the Application Password it creates after your Mac, so your Mac's name appears on your WordPress profile.

2. GitHub, to check for updates
When Quill opens, it asks GitHub for the version number of the latest Quill release, so it can tell you when an update is available. The request contains nothing about you, your site, or your posts. Like any web request, it shows GitHub your IP address, the app version, and your system version, and is covered by GitHub's privacy statement.

3. Anthropic's API (optional)
AI features work only after you add your own Anthropic API key. Quill has no built-in key and makes no AI calls without one. When you add a key during setup, Quill checks it by asking Anthropic for its list of models; that request contains only the key. When you use an AI feature, Quill sends Anthropic:

  • the text you selected, or the whole list or table it is in, when you ask Quill to rewrite it;
  • the description you type when you generate a post;
  • the post's title and full text when you run a Content Evaluation;
  • the full text of your style sample posts when you generate a style guide;
  • your style guide, with every request.

If you turn on Web Search, Anthropic may search the web for your topic while generating a post. Quill does not contact any search engine itself. Everything sent to Anthropic uses your own API key and is subject to Anthropic's privacy policy.

What Quill never does

No analytics or telemetry.

Quill does not collect usage data, crash reports, or any metrics about how you use the app.

No accounts.

There is no Quill account, no sign-in, and no central server. The app has no way to identify you.

No cloud sync.

Your drafts and settings stay on your Mac. Your writing leaves it only when you save it to your WordPress site or send it to Anthropic with an AI feature.

No advertising.

Quill is not ad-supported and shares no data with advertisers.

Deleting your data

To remove everything Quill stores on your Mac:

  • Quit Quill.
  • Delete ~/Library/Application Support/Quill/ — this removes credentials, AI settings, and the local draft database.
  • Delete ~/Library/Preferences/com.siolon.quill.plist, ~/Library/Caches/com.siolon.quill, ~/Library/WebKit/com.siolon.quill, and ~/Library/HTTPStorages/com.siolon.quill.
  • Delete Quill.app from your Applications folder.

Deleting these files does not revoke Quill's access to your site. To do that, delete its Application Passwords in WordPress under Users → Profile. Each browser connection creates one named "Quill on" followed by your Mac's name, so there may be more than one.
