Introduction

Quill is a Mac app for writing and publishing on a self-hosted WordPress site. You write posts and pages in Quill, and it saves them to WordPress in the same block format the WordPress editor uses, so you can move between the two whenever you like.

Quill handles the writing: posts and pages, images, galleries and featured images, categories and tags, scheduling, and optional AI help through Anthropic's Claude API. Themes, plugins, comments, users and site layout stay in the WordPress admin.

Quill is free to use. It is not open source; the terms are in the license.

Requirements and Installation

Requirements

  • A Mac running macOS 27 (Golden Gate) or later. Quill won't launch on earlier versions of macOS.
  • A self-hosted WordPress site running WordPress 7.0 or later, reachable over HTTPS. Older versions of WordPress may work, but they aren't supported.

Installing Quill

Download Quill from quill.siolon.com, unzip it, and drag Quill.app into your Applications folder.

Quill is notarized by Apple. The first time you open it, macOS asks you to confirm that you want to open an app downloaded from the internet. Click Open. You only see this once.

Updates

Quill checks for a new version each time it launches. When there is one, a Quill version available link appears at the top of the sidebar list. Click it to open the release page on GitHub, or click × to hide the notice for that version.

To update, download the new version, unzip it, and replace Quill.app in your Applications folder. Your credentials, drafts and settings are stored separately and carry over.

Help → Changelog lists what changed in each release. Help → User Guide opens this page.

Connecting to WordPress

Quill signs in to WordPress with an application password: a password WordPress generates for a single app, which you can revoke at any time without changing your account password. You usually don't create it yourself. The first time you open Quill, it asks your site for one and WordPress hands it over once you approve.

Connecting on First Launch

  1. Open Quill. Under Site address, type your site's address, such as example.com, and click Continue. Quill adds https:// for you, and it works with or without www.
  2. Your browser opens your site's Authorize Application page. Log in if WordPress asks, then click Yes, I approve of this connection.
  3. When your browser asks whether to open Quill, allow it. Quill finishes connecting.

WordPress names the password after your Mac and the time you connected, so on your WordPress profile you can tell your connections apart and revoke one without affecting the others.

Once connected, Quill offers to add an Anthropic API key for the AI writing features. Paste a key and click Save, or click Skip for Now and add one later (see Setting Up).

Connecting With an Application Password

Some sites turn browser approval off. When yours does, Quill asks for an application password instead. You can also choose Use an application password instead on the first screen, where Open Profile Page takes you straight to the right part of your WordPress profile.

Step 1: Create an Application Password

  1. Log in to your WordPress admin.
  2. Go to Users → Profile.
  3. Scroll down to Application Passwords.
  4. In New Application Password Name, type a name you'll recognize, such as "Quill".
  5. Click Add New Application Password.
  6. Copy the password WordPress shows you. WordPress won't show it again.

Step 2: Add It to Quill

  1. Fill in:
    • Site address: your site's address. Quill uses https://; plain http:// is accepted only for a site running on localhost.
    • Username: your WordPress username.
    • Application password: the password from step 1.
  2. Click Connect.

Quill checks the credentials with your site before saving them. To change your site or credentials later, choose Quill → Settings (⌘,) and edit the fields under WordPress Credentials.

Credentials are stored in a file on your Mac that only your user account can read, not in the system keychain, so Quill never asks for your Mac password.

If Quill Can't Load Your Site

When a section fails to load, the sidebar shows the error with a Retry button (⌘R also retries). If Quill can't reach your site or can't sign in, Open Blog Settings… lets you check the site address, username and application password. Local drafts are stored on your Mac, so the Drafts section keeps working while your site is unreachable.

If the error says WordPress sent a reply Quill couldn't read, something on your site is adding text to its API responses, usually a PHP warning or notice from a plugin or theme. Fix or disable whichever one is producing it.

Quill's Interface

The Quill window has three parts: the sidebar on the left, the editor in the middle, and an inspector on the right. The inspector shows post settings while you're editing, or file details in the media library. You can hide the sidebar and the inspector separately.

The Sidebar

Four buttons at the top of the sidebar switch between its sections:

  • Posts and Pages list everything in that section of your site, including drafts, scheduled, pending and private items, newest first.
  • Drafts lists local drafts: posts and pages that exist only on your Mac and haven't been sent to WordPress yet.
  • Media shows your media library as a grid, with filters for each kind of file in the sidebar.

The search field below the section buttons filters the current list by title. In Posts and Pages, the filter button beside it shows only items with one status, such as Published, Draft or Scheduled; choose All Posts (or All Pages) to turn it off.

Click an item to open it. To remove one, right-click it:

  • Move to Trash sends a post or page to the WordPress trash, where you can restore it from the WordPress admin.
  • Delete Draft deletes a local draft permanently. Local drafts have no trash.

With nothing open, the editor area shows your site's name and two short lists. A scheduled post that didn't go out on time is marked Missed schedule. Click an item to open it.

Writing and Editing

Creating Posts and Pages

Use the File menu or the + menu in the window toolbar:

  • New Post (⌘N)
  • New Page (⇧⌘N)
  • New Media… (⌥⌘N), called Upload Media… in the + menu

A new post or page starts as a local draft in the Drafts section. It stays on your Mac until you send it to WordPress.

Type the title in the Title field at the top of the editor, and the body below it. Headings, lists, quotes and other blocks show their formatting as you write. Your theme's fonts and colors apply only on the published site.

A block you insert goes after the block the cursor is in, as in the WordPress editor, so it never splits a paragraph. On an empty line, it takes that line's place.

The Second Toolbar Row

When the cursor is in a block that has options of its own, such as a table, an image or a link, a second row of controls appears under the toolbar. Each block's controls are listed in its section under Working With Blocks.

Links

Select some text and click the Link button to open the link picker. Type to search your site's posts, pages, categories, tags and media by title, or paste any URL, then click a result or press Return. With the cursor on an existing link, the picker also has a Remove link button.

To follow a link from the editor, hold ⌘ and click it. To make a link open in a new tab, turn on New tab in the second toolbar row.

Markdown Shortcuts

The editor turns common Markdown into formatting as you type.

Blocks

Type these at the start of an empty line:

- or * or + Bulleted list

5. Numbered list starting at 5 (any number works)
> Quote
``` and a spaceCode block
```js and a spaceCode block with a language set (js, python and so on)
---Separator
*** or ___ Separator

Inline Formatting

These convert as soon as you type the closing characters:

Type thisTo get
**bold** or __bold__Bold
*italic* or _italic_Italic
~~strikethrough~~Strikethrough
`code`Inline code

A web address or email address becomes a link when you type a space after it.

If a shortcut converts something you wanted to keep as typed, press Delete straight away and your characters come back.

Quill doesn't change punctuation as you type: -- stays two hyphens, ... stays three dots, and straight quotes stay straight. Type or paste typographic characters if you want them.

Pasting

Text copied from a web page, a word processor or a chat app carries formatting, and ⌘V handles it. Plain-text Markdown needs its own command, Paste as Markdown.

Formatted Text (⌘V)

Quill rebuilds pasted content with its own formatting, so you don't inherit another app's fonts, colors or stray code, and WordPress accepts every block it produces without asking to repair it. Headings, paragraphs, lists, quotes, tables, images, links and inline formatting all come through.

Pasted fromWhat you get
WordReal bulleted and numbered lists, nested as they were
A code editor such as VS CodeOne code block, with its line breaks and indentation
ChatGPT or ClaudeCode blocks keep their code, without the language label or the Copy button
A YouTube, Vimeo or Spotify player on a web pageAn embed of the video or track
Any other <iframe>, such as a mapRemoved. Nothing is inserted

A YouTube, Vimeo, X, Spotify, SoundCloud, TikTok or Instagram link pasted on its own into an empty line becomes an embed. Plain text with numbered lines, such as 1. Preheat the oven and 2. Mix the dough, becomes a numbered list when you paste it into an empty paragraph.

A screenshot, or an image file copied in the Finder, uploads to your media library when you paste it. Pictures inside a pasted Word document upload too. Text you drag in from another app is handled the same way as a paste, according to where you drop it.

Copying and pasting inside Quill keeps every block exactly as it was. After a large paste from elsewhere, it's worth a look in code view to confirm everything arrived.

Markdown (⇧⌘V)

The Markdown Shortcuts only work on text you type. Pasted with ⌘V, most Markdown arrives as plain characters. Choose Edit → Paste as Markdown (⇧⌘V) instead to convert the whole clipboard at once: headings, lists, quotes, tables, code blocks, separators, links and inline formatting.

  • Text copied from a web page or chat app usually pastes better with ⌘V, because it's already formatted.
  • A Markdown image (![alt](url)) keeps its original address, so the published post would load it from wherever it lives now. Replace it with an image uploaded to your media library.

Find and Replace

Press ⌘F to open the find bar. Type in Find to highlight every match, and press Return or ⇧Return to move between them. Turn on Aa to match case. To replace text, type the new text in Replace, then click Replace for the current match or All for every match.

Spell Check

Click ABC in the toolbar to check spelling. Misspelled words get a red underline. Right-click an underlined word to pick a suggestion. Click ABC again to check the post again after editing.

Code View

Click </> in the toolbar to switch between the visual editor and the post's HTML. Code view shows exactly what Quill saves to WordPress, including the block comments such as <!-- wp:paragraph -->.

You can type straight into the HTML, and ⌘S saves what's in code view without leaving it. When you save or switch back, Quill tidies your HTML into the form the WordPress editor expects.

Classes and IDs you add are kept, and so are inline styles on paragraphs, headings, lists, table cells and images. An element the editor doesn't know, such as a custom tag inside a paragraph, is removed when you save, and its text stays. To keep HTML exactly as you wrote it, put it in a Custom HTML block.

Code view is also where you change blocks Quill has no editor for, which appear as cards in the visual editor (see Block Editor Compatibility).

Working With Blocks

A post is a series of blocks: paragraphs, headings, lists, images and the rest. Paragraphs, headings and lists come from the formatting toolbar. The blocks below each have a toolbar button or an entry in the toolbar's + menu, and the controls that appear for them in the second toolbar row.

Moving and Deleting Blocks

Moving

Point at a block and a small marker appears in the margin to its left, showing what kind of block it is. Drag the marker to move the block; an amber line shows where it will land.

To move a heading together with everything under it, hold ⌘ with the pointer on the heading's marker. The section is tinted, up to the next heading of the same or a higher level. Keep holding ⌘ and drag.

Without the mouse, Edit → Move Block Up (⌃⌘↑) and Move Block Down (⌃⌘↓) move the top-level block the cursor is in. Each move is one step for ⌘Z, and footnotes are renumbered to follow the new order.

Leaving a Block

Press Esc to move out of a quote, table, code block, image caption or block from the + menu, onto a new paragraph after it. In a quote or a block from the + menu, Return on an empty last line does the same. This is how you carry on writing after a block at the end of a post.

Deleting a Block

Click ✕ at the right end of the second toolbar row, or press ⇧⌘⌫. This works for images, tables, quotes, code blocks, separators, groups and the blocks from the + menu other than Custom HTML. Delete other blocks, Custom HTML included, as you would text.

Quotes and Pullquotes

Press ⇧⌘B or click Blockquote to turn the current block into a quote, and do the same again to turn it back. It works like the WordPress editor's Transform to Quote: with the cursor in a list, the whole list goes inside the quote, and with several blocks selected, they all go into one quote.

With the cursor in a quote, the second toolbar row has:

  • Cite adds a line at the bottom of the quote for the author or source, or removes it.
  • Plain switches the quote to WordPress's plain quote style.

Press Return on an empty line to leave the quote.

A Pullquote, from the + menu, is a quote set apart from the text around it. As in WordPress, it holds only text and a citation, and it has a Cite button too.

Code Blocks and Preformatted

Click {} or press ⌥⌘C to turn the current block into a code block, or type ``` and a space on an empty line. Type a language straight after the backticks, such as ```js, to set the block's language.

Preformatted, from the + menu, keeps text exactly as typed, including spaces and line breaks, but isn't marked as code.

In both, Tab inserts a tab instead of leaving the block.

Images

Click Image in the toolbar and pick an image from your media library. Upload… in the same window adds one from your Mac first. You can also drag image files from the Finder onto the editor, or paste them; Quill uploads them to your media library and inserts them.

Click an image to select it. An image toolbar appears over it, for its size, alt text and link, and the second toolbar row has:

  • The three alignment buttons, , float the image left with text wrapping around it, center it, or float it right.
  • Rounded gives the image fully rounded corners.
  • ✕ deletes the image (⇧⌘⌫).

Size

  • Drag a corner handle to resize the image in proportion, or an edge handle to stretch it.
  • Type exact pixel sizes in the W and H fields in the image toolbar.
  • For images from your media library, Thumb, Medium, Large and Full switch to the files WordPress made for that image.
  • Reset removes any width and height you set and switches back to the original file.

Alt Text

Type a description in the Alt field in the image toolbar. Screen readers read it out, and search engines use it. If the image already has alt text in the media library, Quill fills it in when you insert the image.

Captions

To add a caption, click the space just below the image and type. Press Return to leave the caption and start a new paragraph. When you insert an image that has a caption in the media library, Quill fills it in.

Linking to the Full-Size Image

Click Link to Full Image in the image toolbar to make the image a link to its original, full-size file. Click it again to remove the link.

Inline Images

An image can also sit inside a line of text. Quill keeps these in posts from the WordPress editor, and adds one itself when you insert an image into a table cell. Click one to set its width and alternative text, then click Apply.

Galleries

Click Gallery in the toolbar to open the gallery picker. Click images to select them; they're added to the Selected list in the order they'll appear. Drag an image in that list by its handle to move it, or click its ✕ to take it out. Upload… adds an image from your Mac.

Click the arrow next to a selected image to give it Alt Text and a Caption for this gallery. The image in your media library isn't changed.

The gallery settings are below the Selected list:

  • Columns: how many images per row, from 1 to 8.
  • Crop: turn on Square to crop every image to a square.
  • Link To: None, or Full Image to make each image a link to its full-size file.
  • Size: which of WordPress's image sizes to use: Thumbnail, Medium, Large or Full Size.

Click Insert Gallery to add the gallery as a single block. To change it later, double-click it or click Edit… under the thumbnails, make your changes and click Update Gallery. Galleries made in the WordPress editor can be edited the same way, and settings the picker doesn't show are kept.

Tables

Click Table in the toolbar to open a size grid, and click the size you want. A new table has a header row. With the cursor in a table, the second toolbar row has:

  • +Row and +Col add a row below or a column to the right. −Row and −Col remove the ones the cursor is in.
  • Header and Footer turn the header and footer rows on or off.
  • Stripes shades every other row.

Press Tab to move to the next cell and ⇧Tab to move back. Tab in the last cell adds a new row. Image with the cursor in a cell adds a small inline image to the cell's text.

Quill keeps a table caption written in the WordPress editor, but can't add or change one; do that in WordPress.

Footnotes

Put the cursor where you want the footnote number and click * in the toolbar. A numbered marker appears at the cursor, and a matching note is added to the list at the bottom of the post for you to type into.

  • Footnotes are numbered in the order they appear, and renumber as you add or delete them.
  • The ↩︎ button after each note takes you back to its marker in the text.
  • To delete a footnote, delete its marker. The note goes with it.
  • Notes can hold inline formatting and links, but not other blocks.

Quill saves footnotes the way current WordPress does, so on your site they look exactly as they would from the WordPress editor.

Embeds

Embeds put a video, social post or audio player from another site into your post.

  1. Click Embed in the toolbar.
  2. Paste the URL of what you want to embed.
  3. Press Return or click Insert.

In the editor, an embed shows as a card with the provider's name and the URL. The embed itself appears only on your published site.

YouTube, Vimeo, X, Instagram, TikTok, Spotify and SoundCloud links are saved the way the WordPress editor saves them. Other URLs are saved as general embeds, and WordPress tries to show them with its own embed support.

Whether an embed shows correctly on your site depends on the provider, and providers change their embed support without notice. X embeds are known to be unreliable.

Columns

Columns put content side by side. Choose Columns from the + menu to add two, and type in each as you would anywhere else. With the cursor in a column, +Col adds a column and −Col removes the one the cursor is in.

Accordion

An accordion is a stack of sections that open and close, each with its own heading. Choose Accordion from the + menu to add one with a single section, then type its heading and the text under it. With the cursor in an accordion, the second toolbar row has:

  • +Item adds a section, and −Item removes the one the cursor is in.
  • Auto-close closes the other sections when a reader opens one.
  • Icon shows or hides the open/close icon. While it's on, Left and Right put it on that side.
  • Open shows the section the cursor is in open when the page loads.

Tabs

Tabs show one panel at a time, with a row of tabs above to switch between them. Choose Tabs from the + menu to add two, named Tab 1 and Tab 2. Click a tab to show its panel, and type over its name to rename it. With the cursor in a tab, the second toolbar row has:

  • +Tab adds a tab, and −Tab removes the current one.
  • Default makes the current tab the one readers see first.

Details

A Details block is a section that opens and closes when the reader clicks its summary line. Choose Details from the + menu, type the summary, then the text under it. Open in the second toolbar row shows it open when the page loads.

Buttons

Choose Buttons from the + menu to add a button, and type its label. With the cursor in a button, the second toolbar row has:

  • Link sets where the button goes, using the same picker as links in text. Once it has a link, New tab makes it open in a new browser tab.
  • +Button adds another button beside it, and −Button removes the current one.
  • Outline switches the current button from filled to outlined.

Separator

A separator is a horizontal line between sections. Choose Separator from the + menu, or type --- on an empty line. Click it to select it, and use the menu in the second toolbar row to pick Default, Wide Line or Dots.

Custom HTML

Choose Custom HTML from the + menu to add HTML of your own, such as an embed code or a styled box. Type or paste it in the window that opens and click Insert (⌘Return). If your WordPress account can add unfiltered HTML (on most sites, administrators and editors), the window also has CSS and JavaScript tabs.

In the editor, the block is a card with a preview of its HTML, which renders only on your site. Double-click the card or click Edit… to change it.

Saving and Publishing

Local Drafts

Click Save Locally or press ⌘S to save a local draft on your Mac. Nothing is sent to WordPress.

To send a local draft to WordPress, choose a Status in Post Settings, then click the publish button or press ⇧⌘P. The button's name tells you what will happen. A new draft's status is Draft, so the button reads Save to WordPress and creates a draft on WordPress that readers can't see. Set the status to Published first if you want it to go live straight away. Once the draft is on WordPress, it moves from Drafts to Posts or Pages.

Posts and Pages on WordPress

⌘S and the publish button both save your changes to WordPress with the post's current status. On a published post, the button reads Update; on a draft, Save Draft. If you set a published post's status back to Draft, the button reads Switch to Draft, and saving takes the post off your site.

Revert throws away your unsaved changes, including changes in Post Settings, and reloads the post from WordPress. Refresh (⌘R) picks up edits made in the WordPress editor, as long as you have no unsaved changes in the post.

Before saving, Quill checks whether the post has been changed on WordPress since you opened it, for example in the WordPress editor. If it has, a Conflict Detected alert gives you three choices: Keep Local saves your version over the one on WordPress, Use Server throws away your changes for good, including the autosave on your Mac, and loads the WordPress version, and Cancel does nothing.

Change Bars

A thin grey bar in the right margin marks each block that differs from the last saved version, and a short grey dash marks where a block was deleted. Hover over one to see what it marks. Saving or reverting clears them.

Autosave

Quill saves your work on your Mac 30 seconds after your last change, and again when you switch to another item or quit. Nothing is sent to your site. If you reopen a post before saving it to WordPress, even after quitting Quill, your changes come back.

Autosave keeps the title, the body and the footnotes, but not Post Settings (apart from a local draft's excerpt). Setting changes you haven't saved to WordPress are lost when you leave the post.

Preview

Click Preview to see a draft in your browser the way it looks on your site, with your unsaved changes. It works only for posts and pages already on WordPress, not for local drafts.

Previewing a draft saves your changes into the draft on WordPress, because WordPress keeps no separate preview copy of a draft.

For a published, scheduled, pending or private post, the button is View Post (View Page for a page). It opens the version saved on WordPress, without your unsaved changes. To see your changes on the site, save them first; for a published post, that updates the live post.

Post and Page Settings

Click Post Settings at the right end of the window toolbar to open the settings inspector for the current post or page. Changes here are sent to WordPress the next time you save or publish, and Revert puts them back along with the rest of the post.

Settings aren't autosaved. Changes you haven't saved to WordPress are lost if you switch to another item or quit. For a local draft, that's every setting except the excerpt.

Status

Status controls whether and when the post is visible on your site:

StatusMeaning
DraftSaved on WordPress but not visible to readers
Pending ReviewWaiting for an editor to approve it
PublishedLive on your site
ScheduledWill be published automatically at the date and time you set
PrivatePublished, but visible only to logged-in administrators and editors
Local draftSaved only on your Mac; not on WordPress yet

Publish Date

Turn on Schedule, or choose Scheduled in the Status menu, to pick a date and time. The publish button then reads Schedule, and WordPress publishes the post at that time. Turn Schedule off to go back to Draft.

If the time you pick has already passed, WordPress publishes the post immediately, and Quill asks you to confirm first. To publish a scheduled post right away, change its Status to Published and save.

Parent Page (Pages only)

Choose a parent to put this page under another one in your site's structure and web address, or leave it at None (top-level).

Categories (Posts only)

Tick a category to assign it, or type in the Filter or add categories… field to find one. To add a new category, type its name and press Return. It's created on your site when you save or publish the post.

Tags (Posts only)

Type in the Filter or add tags… field to find a tag and click it to add it, or press Return to create a new one. Click the × on a tag to remove it. New tags are created on your site when you save or publish the post.

Featured Image

The featured image is the picture your theme shows with the post or page, for example at the top of the post or next to it in lists. Click Choose… to pick one from your media library (Upload… there adds one from your Mac), or drag an image file from the Finder onto the box. Click the image or Replace… to pick a different one, and Remove to clear it.

Alt text belongs to the image, not the post, so change it in the Media Library.

Slug

The slug is the part of the post's web address that comes from its title, such as my-first-post. Change it here, or leave it blank and WordPress makes one from the title.

Excerpt (Posts only)

An excerpt is a short summary that some themes show on archive and search pages. If you leave it blank, your theme may show the opening words of the post there instead. Generating a post with AI fills it in for you.

Discussion

Turn off Allow comments to close comments on the post or page.

Stats

A live word and character count for the post, with an estimated reading time.

Media Library

Click Media at the top of the sidebar to browse everything uploaded to your site, newest first. The filters in the sidebar narrow the grid to one kind of file, and the search field searches within the current filter, so if a file you expect is missing, switch to All Media.

Click an item to select it, and use the arrow keys to move the selection. Press Space or double-click an image for a large preview, and Space or Esc to close it. Right-click an item for these commands:

CommandWhat it does

Details

Select an item and click Media Info at the right end of the window toolbar to see its title, alt text, caption, file name, type, dimensions, size, upload date and URL.

To change the title, alt text or caption, edit the field and press Return or click elsewhere. Quill saves it to WordPress straight away; press Esc instead to back out. A new caption or alt text is used the next time you insert the image, and posts that already use it keep what they were given. Changing the title doesn't rename the file.

Uploading

Choose Upload Media… from the + menu, or File → New Media… (⌥⌘N) from anywhere in Quill. You can upload images, PDFs, Word, Excel and PowerPoint files, and plain-text files such as .txt and .csv. Video and audio files can't be uploaded from Quill; upload them in WordPress and they'll appear here.

HEIC photos, the format iPhones use by default, are converted to JPEG on your Mac before every upload, so WordPress can make its usual smaller sizes. Your original file isn't changed.

Deleting Media

Right-click an item and choose Delete. Quill asks you to confirm first.

Deleting media is permanent. WordPress has no trash for media, so a deleted file can't be recovered.

AI Writing Features

Quill can use Anthropic's Claude API to write posts, rewrite selected text and review your writing. These features are off until you add your own Anthropic API key, and Anthropic bills you directly for what you use.

Setting Up

  1. Create an API key at platform.claude.com/settings/keys.
  2. Choose Quill → Settings (⌘,) and find the AI Writing section.
  3. Paste the key into Anthropic API Key and click Save.

Once a key is saved, the Generate and Evaluate buttons appear in the editor toolbar. To turn the AI features off, delete the key and click Save.

Model and Reasoning

The Model menu lists every Claude model your API key can use, newest first. Until you pick one, Quill uses the newest Claude Haiku, which is fast and inexpensive.

Reasoning sets how long Claude thinks before it answers. Higher levels take more time and cost more. The menu lists only the levels the selected model offers.

Writing Style

Quill can learn how you write from your own posts. In the AI Writing section of Settings, click Choose Posts… and pick up to five posts that sound like you. When you click Save, Claude writes a style guide of about 500 words from them, which Quill uses whenever it writes, rewrites or reviews for you. Click Regenerate to rebuild the guide, for example after switching models.

Pick posts on different topics. When most of them share a subject, the guide mistakes the subject for your style.

Web Search

With Web Search on, which it is to begin with, Claude can search the web for current information while writing a post, and Evaluate checks your facts. Turn it off to work from Claude's own knowledge only, which is faster and costs less.

Generating a Post

Click the Generate button in the toolbar. Describe what you want, anything from a topic to a detailed brief, and click Generate (⌘Return). Quill writes a complete post or page with a title and loads it into the editor, and fills in the post's excerpt. With web search on, Claude looks up anything that may have changed, such as prices and versions, and links each fact it found to its source.

Claude writes in your style but doesn't make things up about you: it won't invent your experiences, work or history unless your description mentions them. Add the personal parts yourself.

If the editor already has content, Quill asks before replacing it. If a long post is cut off before it's finished, Get Full Version asks again with a longer limit, which costs more.

Evaluating Your Writing

Click the Evaluate button in the toolbar to have Claude review the current post or page. It needs at least 100 words. The results open in the inspector in place of Post Settings: what works in the post and the two or three changes that would improve it most, then three lists:

  • Fixes — corrections any copy editor would make: spelling, grammar, punctuation, word choice and inconsistent names or terms.
  • Ideas — judgment calls: a sentence that's hard to follow, wording that could be shorter, a repeated word, or a passage that drifts from your style.
  • Facts to Check — claims worth checking, each with what's wrong, the source's own sentence and a link to the source. This list needs Web Search on.

Click a finding to jump to it in the editor. Click Apply to make the change, or Apply All at the top of Fixes to make every correction; ⌘Z undoes either. Treat fact checks as things to look into: read the source before you change anything. Click Re-evaluate to run the review again, and Post Settings in the window toolbar to get back to your settings.

Rewriting a Selection

Select at least ten characters of text, right-click the selection, and choose one of these:

CommandWhat it does
Make LongerKeeps your sentences and adds new ones that explain the reasoning or give an example, without adding facts or experiences you didn't write
Make ShorterCuts the least important sentences and tightens the rest, keeping your words where it can
RephraseMakes the selection read more clearly, with the same meaning and about the same length
Fix Spelling & GrammarFixes spelling, grammar and punctuation, and nothing else
Convert to TableTurns the selection into a table
Convert to ListTurns the selection into a bulleted list

Links, footnote markers and formatting stay with the words they belong to. You can keep writing elsewhere while Claude works.

The new text replaces your selection, highlighted, and an Accept / Discard bar appears at the bottom of the editor. Accept (or Return) keeps it; Discard (or Esc) puts your original text back.

Limitations and Compatibility

WordPress.com

Quill works only with self-hosted WordPress. WordPress.com sites aren't supported.

One Site at a Time

Quill connects to one WordPress site. To switch to another, change the credentials in Settings. Autosaved changes belong to the site they were made on, and come back only when you connect to that site again. Local drafts aren't tied to a site: the Drafts section shows the same drafts for every site, and a draft is published to whichever site is connected. Your writing style picks are cleared when you switch.

When Quill Can't Keep Something

Each time Quill opens a post, and each time you leave code view or save with it open, it checks that every block in the post is still there in the editor. If any are missing, a red banner at the top of the editor names them, and saving and previewing are blocked until you respond. Saving at that point would write the shortened post over the real one.

When you see the banner:

  1. Check which blocks it names.
  2. If you need them, don't save. Nothing has been written yet. Close the post and edit it in the WordPress editor instead.
  3. To fix it in Quill, open code view (</>) and paste the missing block's markup back in. When you leave code view, the banner goes away and you can save again.
  4. If you don't mind losing them, click Save anyway, I understand and save. WordPress's revision history still has the earlier version.

You should rarely see this banner. Quill normally keeps blocks it can't edit exactly as they were (see below).

Block Editor Compatibility

Quill saves posts in the same block format as the WordPress editor for everything it can edit: paragraphs, headings, lists, quotes, code blocks, separators, images, galleries, tables, embeds, footnotes, links, Custom HTML, and the container blocks (Columns, Details, Buttons, Accordion, Tabs, Group, Pullquote and Preformatted). A post written in Quill opens in the WordPress editor as separate blocks, and you can go back and forth between the two.

Settings you chose in WordPress are kept. A color, a font size, a block style, a link that opens in a new tab, formatting such as subscript or highlight: Quill keeps all of these through an edit even when it has no control for them.

Blocks Quill can't edit, including Audio, Video, Playlist, Cover, Media & Text, Spacer, shortcodes and plugin blocks, appear in the editor as one-line cards showing the block's name. The block is saved back to WordPress exactly as it arrived. You can move or delete a card, and change what's inside it in code view or the WordPress editor.

Groups. A Group, Row, Stack or Grid, such as one from a pattern, opens for editing with a small label above it when everything inside it is something Quill can edit. Quill can't add one, and doesn't draw its colors, spacing or layout, but keeps them exactly as they were. A Group that holds anything Quill can't edit stays a single card.

Synced patterns are shared across posts, so Quill doesn't edit them inside a post. Each one shows as a card with the pattern's title and an Edit in WordPress button.

Classic posts and HTML outside blocks. A post written before the block editor is left alone until you edit it. Then Quill converts it to blocks, the same way WordPress's "Convert to blocks" does. HTML it can't turn into normal blocks, such as a <div class="note"> or an <iframe>, is kept as a Custom HTML block with its markup intact.

What Quill Doesn't Do

These stay in the WordPress admin:

  • Themes and site customization
  • Plugins
  • Comment moderation
  • Users and roles
  • Revision history
  • Image editing, such as cropping or rotating
